Privacy Policy

Effective 17 November 2026

The short version: Waypoint has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. What you log stays on your devices and in your own iCloud, and the developer cannot see it. The only things that leave your device are the food searches and barcode numbers sent to look products up, a single anonymous note when you first set the app up, and anonymous counts of what is used in the app, which never include what you log.

What Waypoint stores

Everything you enter or record in Waypoint: food, water, weigh-ins, workouts, routines, exercise history, check-ins, photos you take or choose in the app, the routes of cardio workouts, your plan, and your profile and goals (such as age, sex, height and targets). Waypoint also keeps the products you have scanned, so a pack you scan again is found at once.

On your device

This data is kept on your iPhone, and on your Apple Watch for workouts recorded there. Your iPhone and Apple Watch pass workouts directly between each other.

In your iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud, Waypoint syncs your data between your devices through Apple's CloudKit, in your private iCloud database. Only you can read it. The developer cannot access it. It is covered by Apple's privacy policy, and you can turn it off at any time in the iOS Settings app under your name, then iCloud.

Weigh-ins that Waypoint reads from Apple Health are not copied to iCloud by Waypoint. They stay on the device, and Apple Health syncs them itself.

Apple Health

If you allow it, Waypoint uses Apple Health as follows:

  • Reads: your age, sex, height and weight, so you do not have to type them in; weigh-ins from other apps and smart scales; active energy, so a day's calorie budget can grow with your activity; and heart rate during workouts.
  • Writes: the food, water, weigh-ins and workouts you log, including the heart rate and energy your Apple Watch measured during a workout.

Data from Apple Health is only used to provide Waypoint's features. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never shared with anyone else. You choose which types Waypoint can read and write, and you can change that at any time in the Health app.

Permissions

  • Camera: to scan barcodes and nutrition labels, and to take a photo for a check-in or after a workout if you choose to. Barcodes and labels are read on your device, and the camera image is never uploaded.
  • Microphone and Speech Recognition: to describe a meal by voice. What you say is turned into words on your iPhone. It is never recorded, kept or uploaded.
  • Photos: when you choose a photo for a check-in or a workout, Waypoint receives only that photo, not your library. When you tap Save on a photo comparison, Waypoint asks to add it to Photos. It can only add, never look at your library.
  • Location: only while you are recording a run, ride or walk that you started, to draw your route and measure distance and pace. The route is saved with that workout.
  • Motion & Fitness: to count steps during a workout you are recording.
  • Notifications: for reminders and alerts such as the end of a rest timer. These are created on your device. Waypoint does not send marketing notifications.

Done on your device

  • Describing a meal: the words you type or say are understood by Apple's on-device intelligence. They are not uploaded. Foods it cannot find in Waypoint's built-in table are looked up in a food database, as described below.
  • Importing workouts: a file you export from another app (such as Hevy or Strong) and choose to import is read on your device.
  • Sharing: a photo or summary leaves Waypoint only when you tap Share, through the iOS share sheet, to where you choose.
  • Widgets and Live Activities: show your figures on your Home Screen, Lock Screen and Apple Watch, from data on the device.

Services outside Waypoint

Food databases

When you search for a food, describe a meal, or scan a barcode, the search text or barcode number is sent to the food databases Waypoint uses, fatsecret and Open Food Facts, to look the product up. Barcode numbers pass through Waypoint's own lookup service on the way to fatsecret; it holds the app's fatsecret key and keeps no logs. Nothing that identifies you is included beyond what any internet request carries, such as your IP address, and Waypoint keeps no record of these lookups.

New-user count

When you finish setting up Waypoint, the app sends one message to Waypoint's own service saying someone new has started, with your App Store country. It includes nothing about you or what you log, and is sent only once. The service keeps no logs and passes the count to the developer as a notification through Pushover.

Usage counts

To help improve the app, Waypoint sends anonymous counts of which features are used. They never include your name, a device ID, or anything you log, and can't be linked to you.

Apple

Waypoint uses Apple services for iCloud sync, Apple Health and downloading exercise videos from the App Store. If you have chosen to share crash reports and diagnostics with app developers in iOS settings, Apple provides anonymous crash reports to the developer.

Waypoint Coach is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and Waypoint only learns whether your Apple Account owns Coach, not who you are or how you paid.

What Waypoint does not do

  • No accounts or sign-ups.
  • No third-party analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites.

Deleting your data

  • On your device: deleting the app removes its data from that device.
  • In iCloud: in the iOS Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, then manage your storage, and delete Waypoint's data.
  • In Apple Health: in the Health app, open your profile, then Apps, then Waypoint, and delete the data it saved.

Children

Waypoint is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes
